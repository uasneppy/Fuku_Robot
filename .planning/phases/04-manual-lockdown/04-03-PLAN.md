<objective>
Anyone who joins a locked group is removed at once and stays out until the lift, and the lift lets them back. A join guard at handler group -7 records each joiner from the chat_member update and ends update handling, so no welcome or captcha runs. A background worker bans recorded joiners through a fleet-wide pacer, with a distinctive end date that marks the ban as the lockdown's own. After /unlockdown restored the permissions, the worker unbans exactly the lockdown's own bans and posts one tally.

Task 1 is the tracer: join, record, ban. Task 2 is the lift side: unban own bans, keep deliberate ones, tally.

Purpose: ROADMAP Phase 4 success criterion 1 (joiners removed at once, can rejoin after the lift), LOCK-01, D-04, D-05, D-10, D-15, D-19, D-21.
Output: lockdown_guard.go, lockdown_marker.go, lockdown_worker.go, the joiner repository, main.go wiring, locale keys and AGENTS.md rules (handler group -7, alita:lockdown:* keys, the worker drain).
</objective>

## Phase Goal

**As a** group admin, **I want to** lock my group down during a raid and lift it when safe, **so that** attackers are stopped and settings survive.

**This slice:** raiders who join during a lockdown are banned on arrival without a welcome, and only they are let back in when it lifts.

## Decisions taken here (Claude's discretion, recorded)

- Handler group -7: before fed-ban (-6) and antiraid (-5), and not shared with antiraid's handler, because gotgbot stops a group at its first matching handler. A banned joiner is handled once, by the lockdown.
- Record then act: the guard only writes the row and returns; the worker does every Telegram write. A paced call inside a handler could pin a dispatcher goroutine for up to 60 s during a raid (research Pattern 3, Anti-Patterns).
- The worker is DB-driven: every replica runs one, claims rows with conditional updates, and wakes on a 2 s tick or when its own guard records a joiner. Its state is all in PostgreSQL, so a restart only resumes (plan 04-06 covers stale claims).
- The lockdown's own ban is a ban until `ban_until`, 330 days after the joiner was recorded (research Pattern 5, Open Question 5): inside Telegram's finite-ban window (over 30 s, under 366 days). The lift compares the live `until_date` with a 2-second tolerance (research A2): a deliberate ban has another end date or none.
- Pacing: a separate `TelegramPacer` with keys `alita:lockdown:pace:next` and `alita:lockdown:pace:block`, 100 ms interval, MaxWait 60 s, so staff fan-outs keep their own budget. A slot past MaxWait fails as rate limited and the row is retried without counting an attempt; other errors count, and the third failure is final.
- Fail open on the guard's own reads and writes: a failed lockdown read or joiner insert lets the joiner in, still muted by the locked default permissions (CONTEXT discretion "never ban without recording first").
- A joiner the guard recorded just as the lift started is let go: the guard re-reads the lockdown after its insert and removes its own pending row when the lockdown is no longer active; the lift cancels any pending row it finds.
- The tally lists at most 25 joiners who could not be unbanned, then "and N more" (`lockdownTallyListMax`), in recording order.

## Assumptions recorded

- A2 (research): `getChatMember` reports `until_date` as sent. The 2-second tolerance absorbs small rounding; the UAT human check below saves a real answer as a fixture.
- A5 (research): `chat_member` updates reach the bot when it is an admin (`AllowedUpdates` already lists `chat_member`). Join service messages and join requests are handled in plans 04-04 and 04-05.
