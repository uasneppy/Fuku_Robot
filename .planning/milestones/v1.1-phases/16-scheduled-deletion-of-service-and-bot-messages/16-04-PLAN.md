## Phase Goal

**As a** group owner, **I want** Telegram's service messages and the bot's own messages removed on a schedule I set, **so that** the chat stays readable.

After this plan every queued row is carried out: deleted in batches at its time, retried when Telegram is busy, dropped quietly when the message is gone or too old, resumed after a restart, done once across replicas, stopped with the process inside the shutdown budget, and the bot's right to delete is classified in one place.

<objective>
Add the deletion worker and its shutdown, the delete-right classifier and live reader, the `main.go` registration and the AGENTS.md startup arithmetic (D-06, D-07, D-09, D-10, D-20, D-22, D-23, D-17 for the reader; research S-01, S-05).

Purpose: rows written by the watcher (16-03) and by the hooks (16-05, 16-06) only matter once something deletes them; the right reader is built here because the worker's service latch needs it and the page (16-08) reuses it unchanged.
Output: two production files, small edits to `deletion_schedule.go` and `main.go`, test files, the shutdown test, AGENTS.md.
</objective>

- The worker's loop context derives from `deletionContext()` (plan 16-03), the root that `StopDeletionWorker` cancels, so one cancel ends the loop, every Telegram call of a cycle and every inline goroutine. `restoreDeletionWorker` (plan 16-03's test helper) puts a fresh root back for the next test.
- The cycle follows the research S-05 order exactly: `ReleaseStale(now - deletionStaleClaim)`; `ClaimDue(now, deletionBatch, token)`; drop rows whose `sent_at` is older than `deletionDropAge` (47 h, D-07 and D-20) with `Complete`; group by chat in sorted order; per chat the latch (a `gone` value drops the chat's claimed rows, a `service` value drops its `service:*` rows and lets `bot:*` through), then a fresh Staff Group read through `deletionStaffLookup` (a row drops the chat's rows with no call; an error releases them free); per chunk of at most `deletionChunk` IDs one `DeleteMessagesWithContext` through `deletionPaced`; success completes the chunk; failure is classified by `deletionClassify` (plan 16-03): `retryFree` releases every remaining claimed row free and ends the cycle, `gone` sets the `gone` latch and drops the chat's claimed and pending rows (`DropChatPending`), anything else falls back to per-ID `DeleteMessageWithContext` each classified on its own (`done` and `refused` complete the row quietly, `retry` releases it counted with `deletionMaxAttempts` 3, `retryFree` as above). `deletionCycleGuard` (90 s) releases unfinished rows free before a cycle returns past it.
- The service latch decision (research S-05 and D-22): when a `service:*` ID is refused with a message-level answer, at most once per chat per cycle one `deletionReadBotRight` decides; `cannot` sets the `service` latch and drops the chat's pending `service:*` rows (`DropChatPending(chat, "service:")`), `can` and `unknown` only drop the row. The worker has no chat type, so it calls the classifier with the administrator flag NOT trusted (an administrator is `can`): an administrator without the right in a supergroup is then never latched and costs one refused call per notice (accepted, bounded by the pacer), which avoids a false latch in a basic group whose administrator may read `can_delete_messages` false (research A2).
- The classifier is the shape of `memberReqClassifyBot` (memberreq_lookup.go:504 to 523): with an error, a Telegram 400 or 403 (`cache.IsBotGoneError`, never when `errors.Is(err, ratelimit.ErrRateLimited)`) is cannot and everything else unknown; with no error the record by status: creator can, administrator can when `CanDeleteMessages` is true or when the flag is not trusted, member, restricted, left and kicked cannot, a null record or any other status unknown. `deletionReadBotRight(ctx, b, chat, timeout, trustAdminFlag)` goes through `deletionPaced` around `b.GetChatMemberWithContext(callCtx, chat.Id, b.Id, nil)` under the caller's timeout; the page passes `chat.Type != "group"` for the flag (a basic-group administrator may read false) and 3 s.
- `deletionWorkerStopWait` is 3 s, an internal wait shorter than the 10 s default, so no `RegisterHandlerWithTimeout` is needed and no drain is registered (D-10, D-23). The one `Release` write in flight when the cancel arrives is bounded by its own detached 5 s database context, so a cut cycle loses nothing: rows are pending again or still claimed and released by the 2-minute stale release.
- The `fuku/modules` harness runs on SQLite here, where concurrent writers can answer "database is locked"; the two-replica and stale-claim tests run their cycles one after the other or behind a barrier the way `TestMemberReqOnePunishmentAcrossReplicas` does (read it first and copy its handling), never by loosening an assertion.
- The shared fakes record any method, so `deleteMessages` and `deleteMessage` are recorded; the batch test reads `params["message_ids"].([]int64)`. The environment gains a scriptable failure per method and per ID (an error, a hang until the request context ends, a count) built on the fake's existing hooks.
- `main.go` is not gofmt-clean at `8c0c827`: `gofmt -l` lists it before and after; ignore it only if it is the only file listed and say so in the SUMMARY.
- Test hygiene as in 16-03: fresh IDs, `deletionCleanup`, `restoreDeletionWorker` in every test that stops the worker, every swapped timing variable through `mreqSwap`, no parallel tests, no skipped cases.
